# Venture studio for athletes and entrepreneurs: feasibility (draft)

Status: a feasibility note only. **Nothing here is built, offered or promised.** It is a separate business line from
Tidy Property Operations and must not share its product, accounts or data. Every structure below needs advice from a
South African corporate lawyer and tax adviser before anyone is approached.

## 1. The idea

Tidy Enterprise partners with athletes (who bring audience and reputation) and entrepreneurs (who bring an operating
business) to build ventures together. Tidy brings systems (TEBOS: operations, records, reporting) and operating help.
The parties share in the result through equity, profit-sharing, or both.

## 2. Questions that decide whether it is viable

1. **Who carries the risk and the work?** A venture studio usually funds early work. Tidy's current position (zero
   added spend) means the studio would contribute systems and time, not cash, until that changes.
2. **What is each party's contribution worth, and how is it measured?** Equity for work ("sweat equity") must be tied to
   defined deliverables and vesting, or it causes disputes.
3. **Can a venture run without the athlete or founder's daily involvement?** If not, the value leaves when they do.
4. **Does Tidy have the capacity?** Each venture competes with Tidy's own products for the founder's time.

## 3. Structures to compare (for legal review)

| Structure | How it works | Main risks |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary shares in a private company (Companies Act 71 of 2008) | Each party holds shares; voting follows shares unless the MOI says otherwise | sweat-equity valuation; tax on shares received for services |
| Shares with vesting (shareholders' agreement) | Shares are earned over time or on milestones; unvested shares can be bought back | needs careful drafting; good-leaver and bad-leaver terms |
| Profit-sharing agreement, no equity | A share of defined profits for a defined period | "profit" must be defined precisely; no say in decisions unless agreed |
| Revenue share | A percentage of revenue | simple to measure; can starve the business of cash |
| Phantom shares or options | Cash value linked to share value, without voting | taxed as income; complex to value |

## 4. Non-negotiable rules

- **Everything disclosed.** Who owns what, who votes how, and who gets paid what is written down and given to every
  party. No undisclosed control arrangements, nominee holdings or concealed beneficial ownership (the Companies Act
  requires beneficial-ownership records for companies).
- **Voting rights match what people are told.** No share classes or side agreements that quietly give control to one
  party.
- **Independent advice.** Every athlete and entrepreneur is told in writing to take their own legal and financial
  advice before signing, and given time to do so.
- **No investment solicitation from the public.** Offering shares to the public has prospectus requirements; this
  model is private agreements only, and any offering is reviewed first.
- **No pressure on athletes.** Many are early in their careers; terms must be fair on their face, not only legal.
- Founder approval for every agreement, valuation and commitment (founder's operating rules).

## 5. Recommendation

Do not launch until Tidy Property Operations has paying pilots. Then run one pilot venture with written terms drafted by
a lawyer, a fixed scope, vesting tied to milestones, and a review after six months. Measure: did the venture reach the
milestones, and did it take less of the founder's time than planned?
