# Property Revenue Architecture: the monthly price (decision 10)

A recommendation for the founder, 19 Oct 2026. **The price is the founder's decision.** Nothing here is published,
charged or put in a contract until the founder says so.

## What is being priced

The monthly subscription for a property owner:
- their cases kept current in the PRA workspace;
- what was done this month;
- the figures then and now, against the frozen baseline.

The first assessment is scoped already (rental income, occupancy and costs). This decision is the monthly price, and
whether there are tiers.

**What this means:**
- Software alone sits at about R180 per unit a month. A managing agent takes about R958 per unit.
- PRA is advice and measurement on top of whatever management the owner already has. So it should sit **between those
  two**, and well under the agent's fee for one property.
- No source gave a going rate for South African landlord revenue advisory, so there is no direct competitor price.

## Recommendation

**Flat monthly tiers by number of units, excluding VAT:**

| Tier | Units | Monthly | Per unit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single | 1 | R750 | R750 |
| Portfolio | 2–5 | R1,950 | R975 down to R390 |
| Portfolio plus | 6–15 | R3,950 | R658 down to R263 |
| Larger | 16 or more | Quoted | — |

**Why flat tiers, not a percentage of rent:**
- Owners can compare a flat price easily.
- A percentage needs verified rent figures every month, and TEBOS treats unknown as unknown.
- A performance fee needs measured outcomes. There are none yet: there are 0 cases, and the product is at stage 2
  without use (ADR 0005). Revisit at stage 3, once outcomes are measured.

**The value test:**
- Single costs R9,000 a year.
- Avoiding one empty month a year on an average rental is worth R9,582.
- Use this in conversation only as arithmetic. It is never a promise, and never a result until a case has measured it.

**Founding clients:**
- the first five clients get 40% off for six months;
- in return, they give written consent for their measured results to be used anonymously.

That produces the stage-3 evidence the portfolio needs.

## Checks before the first invoice

1. **Registration.** Ask the PPRA, or an attorney, whether advising landlords on rental income for a fee makes you a
   property practitioner who needs an FFC. Don't charge until that is answered.
2. **VAT.** Check whether Tidy Enterprise is VAT-registered. If it is, add VAT to the prices above.
3. **The agreement.** It needs:
   - the scope;
   - what is *not* included (no rent collection, no handling of client money);
   - notice;
   - how data is kept.

   It is a legal term, so it is the founder's to approve.
